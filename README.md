Retail Business Analytics & Customer Analytics

Project Overview

This project analyzes historical retail transaction data to understand sales performance, product revenue, customer purchasing behavior, customer retention, and geographic revenue concentration.

Using Python, SQL Server, and Power BI, the project transforms raw transaction records into meaningful business insights and interactive dashboards to support data-driven decision-making.

Business Objectives

Analyze overall sales performance and monthly revenue trends.

Identify top-performing products by revenue and quantity sold.

Compare sales performance across countries.

Calculate total revenue, order volume, and average order value.

Identify repeat customers and one-time customers.

Perform cohort-based customer retention analysis.

Segment customers using Recency, Frequency, and Monetary (RFM) analysis.

Evaluate revenue concentration among high-value customers and countries.

Develop actionable recommendations to improve sales and customer retention.

Technology Stack

Data Cleaning and Preparation

Python and Pandas were used to prepare the transaction data for analysis.

The cleaning process included:

Removing duplicate transaction records.

Converting invoice dates to datetime format.

Excluding cancelled invoices identified by invoice numbers beginning with C.

Removing records with non-positive quantities or unit prices.

Calculating transaction revenue using quantity multiplied by unit price.

Retaining transactions with missing CustomerIDs for sales-level analysis while excluding them from customer-level analyses.

After cleaning, the dataset contained 524,878 transaction rows.

SQL Analysis

SQL Server was used to perform the following analyses:

Overall sales performance: Total revenue, distinct orders, unique customers, and average order value.

Monthly sales analysis: Revenue and order volume by month.

Product performance: Top products ranked by revenue and units sold.

Country performance: Revenue rankings and country-level revenue contribution.

Revenue per order: Comparison of revenue and order counts across countries.

Customer purchasing behavior: Repeat customers versus one-time customers.

Cohort retention: Customer purchasing activity after the first purchase month.

Customer revenue concentration: Revenue generated by the top 10% of known customers.

Month-over-month analysis: Revenue changes and growth percentages using the LAG() window function.

Customer recency analysis: Identification of customers with older last-purchase dates.

Business Interpretation

Monthly sales trends: November 2011 recorded the highest monthly revenue at approximately 1.50 million. February 2011 recorded the lowest at approximately 522,546.

Geographic concentration: The United Kingdom generated approximately 84.59% of total revenue, indicating substantial dependence on one market.

Repeat purchasing: The analysis identified 2,845 repeat customers and 1,493 one-time customers among customers with known IDs.

Customer revenue concentration: The top 10% of known customers generated 61.45% of known-customer revenue.

Product revenue: Postage and service-related entries appeared among the highest-revenue descriptions, so these should be evaluated separately from merchandise when assessing product performance.

Metric Definitions and Limitations

Average order value (AOV) is total cleaned revenue divided by the number of distinct invoices. It measures average revenue per invoice, not average revenue per customer.

Sales-level metrics include transactions with missing CustomerIDs. Customer-level analyses exclude transactions without known CustomerIDs. Therefore, the revenue totals used in these analyses differ.

The retention analysis measures observed repeat purchasing by cohort and month. A customer not appearing in a later month should not automatically be considered permanently lost.

The dataset covers December 2010 through December 2011. Monthly changes are observations from this historical period and do not independently establish recurring seasonality.

The results describe this dataset and should not be interpreted as current market performance.

Business Recommendations

1. Prepare for Peak Sales Periods

November 2011 recorded the highest monthly revenue, at approximately 1.50 million.

Recommendation: Plan inventory, staffing, and promotional campaigns ahead of high-demand periods. Validate the pattern against additional years of data before treating it as recurring seasonality.

2. Improve Performance During Slower Months

February 2011 recorded the lowest monthly revenue, at approximately 522,546.

Recommendation: Test targeted promotions, seasonal offers, and customer engagement campaigns during slower periods. Measure their impact on revenue and order volume.

3. Strengthen Customer Retention

Repeat customers accounted for approximately 93.09% of revenue associated with customers whose IDs were known.

Recommendation: Reward Champions, investigate purchasing declines among At Risk customers, and encourage one-time customers to make repeat purchases.

4. Diversify Geographic Revenue

The United Kingdom contributed approximately 84.59% of total revenue.

Recommendation: Evaluate opportunities in other countries, starting with established markets such as the Netherlands, EIRE, Germany, and France. Test targeted campaigns before committing to large-scale expansion.

5. Manage Customer Revenue Concentration

The top 10% of known customers generated 61.45% of known-customer revenue.

Recommendation: Maintain strong relationships with high-value customers while broadening the customer base and encouraging more customers to become repeat purchasers.
